# Crimson Coast art bible

The island used to read as one town: the same brick and plaster blocks, the same lamps and the
same pavement everywhere. Each zone below now has its own architecture, palette, roofs, street
furniture, planting, paving and night light. Names match the HUD (`zoneName()` in terrain.js), and
the code lives in `public/game/js/districts.js` (the style table) plus terrain.js / world.js /
dressing.js. Borders are jittered with noise so neighbourhoods blend over about 100 m instead of
meeting on a ruler-straight line.

Almost every built-up lot sits between 5 and 25 m above the sea, so contrast comes from the
buildings, planting and light rather than from the terrain.

## Downtown (west centre, around the financial core)
Glass and stone financial district. Blue-grey curtain-wall towers and pale stone office slabs,
getting taller towards the core (towers up to about 150 m), with mid-rise offices at the edges.
Flat roofs with plant rooms and parapets, lobbies and shops at street level. Pale granite paving,
modern cool-white LED lamps, billboards on the main roads. Night: a cold white glow and lit office
floors. Landmark: Crimson Tower, the tallest building on the island, with a spire and a red
aircraft beacon.

## Kingsbay (north-west old town)
Pre-war brownstone and red-brick walk-ups, 3 to 6 storeys, with cornices and dark shopfronts.
Deep red and brown brick with brownstone fronts, flat roofs crowded with wooden water tanks.
Brick paving, black cast-iron lantern lamps with warm light, street trees. Landmark: the skyline
of rooftop water tanks.

## Old Harbour (west hook around the lagoon)
Victorian dockland. Red-brick industrial warehouses with big multi-pane steel windows, painted
ghost signs and loading doors, plus corrugated sheds. Dark worn paving, orange sodium lamps,
dumpsters. Landmark: old dockside cranes at the waterfront.

## Kingz Turf (purple gang turf between Kingsbay and Old Harbour)
Run-down tenements: grimy brick and concrete, barred and boarded windows, fire escapes, roll-down
shutters and graffiti on every ground floor. Purple awnings, chain-link fences, no street trees,
sparse orange lamps.

## Northside (north peninsula)
Leafy suburb. Pastel clapboard houses with shutters, grey gable and hip roofs, picket fences and
hedges, mailboxes at the kerb, small two-storey shopping parades. Big round street trees, cobra
lamps with warm light, greener ground. Landmark: a water tower painted NORTHSIDE.

## North Isle (north-east island)
Fishing village. Weatherboard cottages in white, slate blue and barn red with steep dark roofs,
post-and-rail fences, iron lamps. Landmark: a red-and-white lighthouse with a sweeping beam at
night.

## Mt. Ember (the mountain and its foothill town)
Alpine resort. Timber chalets on stone bases with balconies, shutters and steep dark gable roofs,
chalet hotels in town, pines instead of street trees, dry stone walls, iron lamps with warm light.
Landmark: a chairlift with pylons climbing to the summit.

## Eastgate (east-coast midtown)
Dense nightlife and market quarter. Vivid plaster blocks (teal, coral, yellow, white) and concrete
offices, 4 to 9 storeys, a shop on every ground floor, vertical neon blade signs and rooftop
billboards. Night: magenta and cyan neon. Landmark: a big rooftop neon sign reading EASTGATE.

## Port Vale (east island strip)
Container port and industry. Corrugated warehouses in blue, grey, green and rust with roller
doors, a few brick works, stacked shipping containers on the quays, chain-link fences, tall
sodium lamps and concrete paving. Landmark: ship-to-shore gantry cranes.

## Viper Blocks (red gang turf in south Port Vale)
Sun-faded stucco low-rise, graffiti, shutters and barred windows, red awnings, chain-link fences.

## Pinewood Hills (central hills)
Rich enclave. White modernist villas with floor-to-ceiling glass and timber cladding, Spanish
Revival mansions with terracotta roofs, hedges and privacy walls, palms. Iron lamps. Landmark: the
PINEWOOD sign in giant white letters on the hillside.

## Lakeside and Mirror Lake (centre, around the lake)
Old-money Georgian. Red-brick and cream stucco townhouses with stone lintels, slate hip roofs,
hedges and brick garden walls, mature trees, iron lantern lamps, pale granite paving.

## Westwood (west coast)
Mid-century car town. Single-storey strip malls in beige, mint and peach with tall fascias,
ranch houses with low hip roofs, palms, billboards. Cobra lamps. Landmark: a googie pylon sign
with a starburst.

## Southport (south-west)
Spanish Mission town. White and cream stucco with arched windows, terracotta hip roofs on most
buildings, palms, wrought-iron lamps, terracotta-red paving, warm ground. Landmark: a white
mission bell tower with a tiled cap.

## Coral Bay (south-east beachfront)
Pastel art deco. Pink, mint, aqua, lemon and lilac blocks with rounded corners, eyebrow ledges,
vertical fins and neon trim, deco hotel towers, palm-lined boulevards, pink terrazzo paving.
Night: pink and teal neon. Landmark: a pier with a lit ferris wheel.

## Red Dust Flats (south-west desert)
Run-down desert town. Low adobe buildings with rounded corners, vigas and small deep windows,
trailers, adobe walls and chain-link, cacti, dusty pavements and faded roads, wooden-pole lamps
with orange light. Landmark: a water tower painted RED DUST.

## Saltmarsh (south wetland)
Bayou stilt country. Weathered grey timber shacks with rusty tin gable roofs, post-and-rail
fences, wooden-pole lamps.

## The Spit (south island)
Beach town. Brightly painted clapboard beach houses (turquoise, coral, yellow), low white gable
roofs, palms, rail fences.

## Countryside (everything rural)
Farm belt. White farmhouses with red barns and silos, post-and-rail fences, stone walls and
hedgerows along the lanes.
